## What one trade costs after you cross the spread

Market orders pay the taker fee twice, once to open and once to close. That is 10 basis points per round trip on OKX against 12 on Bitget, or $5 versus $6 on a $10,000 fill. The fee is only part of the bill, though. Slippage, the price you move by eating through the order book, grows with size, and third-party measurement against each venue's live book puts the all-in figure at **$10.02 on OKX versus $12.02 on Bitget for a $10,000 BTC market order**, and **$1,129 versus $1,350 at the $1,000,000 size**.

Limit orders change the arithmetic. A resting order pays the maker fee instead, and at 0.02% on both exchanges the gap disappears entirely at the base tier. If you post liquidity rather than take it, the fee argument for OKX evaporates and you are choosing on markets, leverage and jurisdiction alone.

## Security: two very different kinds of bad news

Neither exchange has lost customer assets in a custody breach. What each has on its record looks nothing like the other's.

OKX's incidents are compliance and operational. On 16 October 2020 it suspended withdrawals for **41 days**, until 26 November, while private-key holders cooperated with Chinese police; no user funds were lost. In February 2025 its operating entity pleaded guilty in the US to running an unlicensed money transmitting business and agreed to more than **$504 million** in penalties, made up of a $420.3 million forfeiture and an $84.4 million fine, with an external compliance consultant retained through February 2027. The same period brought a roughly €1.05 million fine from Malta's FIAU and €2.25 million from the Dutch central bank, and on 11 September 2026 Texas issued a consent order against OKX Inc. None of these involved user balances being taken. OKX also has no published insurance fund size, and in October 2026 launched OKX Shield, a voluntary compensation programme.

Bitget's record includes a direct theft. In **September 2026, about $388 million** was taken from hot and warm wallets after attackers used a flaw in a third-party security product to obtain high-privilege internal credentials, hitting 12 addresses across 11 chains. The cold wallet was untouched, the company said the loss was covered by corporate funds and its protection fund, and account balances were unaffected. Withdrawals were suspended for 4 days for BTC, 6 days for USDT and 8 days for all assets. The protection fund carries an official commitment of at least **$300 million**. Earlier marks on the record include a BGB flash crash in 2024, the April 2025 VOXEL/USDT perpetual incident in which trades were rolled back and compensation pledged, and warnings from Japan's FSA in March 2023 and November 2024.

Proof of reserves differs in method as well as number. OKX publishes a monthly Merkle tree with zk-STARK verification: on 8 September 2026 it showed BTC at 109%, ETH at 101% and USDT at 105%. Bitget publishes monthly figures itself with no third-party audit: on 29 September 2026, media reports put BTC at 142%, ETH at 110% and USDT at 107%, with a total reserve ratio of 131%. The two are not directly rankable. One published comparison set OKX's primary assets at $22.96 billion against Bitget's 122% aggregate ratio across four tracked assets, which measure different things and cannot establish relative solvency. Reserve reports test whether reported balances exist, not whether every liability is disclosed.
